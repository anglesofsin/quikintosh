# Media, music, text and live lyrics

Open **Edit Widgets** from the desktop context menu. Add a widget, then right-click it to edit its content, resize it, or change its appearance. Existing Image widgets become **Media** without losing their files, positions or styles.

## Media

Add multiple photos, GIFs and videos, or choose an entire local folder. Folder libraries support optional subfolders, alphabetical/newest ordering and shuffle. Choose 15 seconds, one minute, 15 minutes or a custom interval. Transitions include dissolve, slide, zoom and cut, with adjustable duration. Videos can finish before advancing; sound starts disabled and has an independent volume control. Fill, fit, stretch and shape controls remain available.

Folder discovery runs outside the UI thread and refreshes periodically while active. Libraries are bounded to 3,000 files; video posters use a bounded disk cache. Previews and glass snapshots never create video/audio players.

## Music

The new **Music** cards play a local audio file. Choose a square cover/card or a rotating record; set the cover, title, artist, description, font and colors. Embedded title/artist/album metadata fills empty fields when a file is selected. Configure volume, mute, repeat, automatic playback and visible controls.

Music and media videos play only while the desktop is focused. Opening another app, locking the session, or opening the widget gallery/editor pauses them. They resume when the desktop regains focus unless manually paused. Gallery previews never produce sound.

## Styled text

Choose Modern, Classic, Rounded, Typewriter, Serif, Handwriting or Neon styling, or enter an installed font family. Customize size, weight, italic, spacing, case, alignment, padding, rotation and entrance animation. Effects include solid, gradient, shadow, outline, neon and glass lettering. Highlights and custom colors are optional. Follow the shell appearance or use the wallpaper accent generated by the existing Matugen pipeline; the normal widget surface controls include Liquid Glass and transparent styles.

## Live lyrics

**Live Lyrics** follows the shell's active MPRIS player, including Spotify clients that publish track metadata and playback position. Online lyrics use the existing LRCLIB integration. Choose a current-line, surrounding-lines or line-progress layout; customize typography and colors and adjust synchronization offset. A local timestamped `.lrc` file can replace the online source. **Hide text when lyrics are unavailable** starts enabled: no track, no lyric, disabled lyrics or an in-progress unsuccessful lookup leaves the widget text empty, including its song title. Disable the option to see status messages.

Availability and timing depend on the track's lyrics and the player's published metadata. Untimed lyrics are identified as untimed; word-level timing is not invented. Local music widgets play their own files; the live lyrics widget follows the active MPRIS player rather than publishing a second player.

## References and validation

Visual direction: [Instagram stickers](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/05/new-stickers-in-instagram-stories/) and [Instagram text styles and animation](https://engineering.fb.com/2022/07/18/developer-tools/building-text-animations-for-instagram-stories/). These are native Quickintosh components, not Instagram assets.

Automated checks:

```sh
python3 -m unittest discover -s modules/lickintosh/creative-widgets -p test_media.py
QUICKINTOSH_TEST_QS=/path/to/qs python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_creative_widgets.py
```

Additional isolated Wayland validation exercised real Qt audio/video playback, pause/resume and lock gating, slideshow advancement, forward/backward LRC seeks, native file/folder picker ownership, multi-file/folder accepted handlers and metadata extraction. Audio was muted during automated playback checks. This does not establish codec/audio-device support on every target machine or online lyric availability for every song.
